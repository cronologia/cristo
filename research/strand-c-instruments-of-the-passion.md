# cronologia/cristo — Strand C: Instruments of the Passion and associated objects

Research-only report. Fetch date: 2026-09-24. No repo files touched.
All quotes were copied from pages fetched this session, and the fetched URL is named each time. Where I could not verify a claim, it is marked **(to verify)**. Disagreements are marked **[DISAGREEMENT]**.

**How ADR-0007 is applied.** Each "formal Church act" line names one authority's act about one named object and cites the document. Expositions, venerations, jubilees, indulgences, papal visits and masses, feasts, custody assignments and gifts are recorded as **adjacent** (acts of cult). They are not findings of authenticity. For every relic I searched for a document in which a Church authority declares the object authentic, and I found none. The closest are two acts that make a cult official: Borromeo in Monza in 1576 (as the museum reports it) and the Apostolic Penitentiary in Valencia in 2015. Both are cult acts.

## 1. True Cross

**Location as of 2026:**
- **Rome.** Santa Croce in Gerusalemme, Cappella delle Reliquie. The basilica's own page, fetched at https://www.santacroceroma.it/it/features-2/reliquie.html, says the relics "nel 1931 trovarono la loro definitiva collocazione nell'attuale Cappella". It also carries a dated notice: *"16.04.2026 Si informa che su disposizione del Vicariato di Roma e del Ministero dei Beni Culturali … la Cappella delle reliquie è temporaneamente chiusa per lavori urgenti."* **So on the fetch date the relic chapel was closed.**
- **Paris.** Notre-Dame de Paris treasury. The Diocese of Paris says a 24 cm fragment "provient lui aussi de celui conservé au trésor de la Sainte-Chapelle… sauvé par un membre de la Commission temporaire des Arts qui le remettra à Notre-Dame en 1805". Source: https://dioceseparis.fr/-la-sainte-couronne-d-epines-de-.html
- **Jerusalem, Holy Sepulchre (Franciscan / Latin).** The Custody of the Holy Land says that each 7 May "the relic of the wood of the Cross of Christ being carried in procession by the Franciscan Father Custos to the point where, according to tradition, it was found". Source: https://www.custodia.org/en/sanctuaries/basilica-of-the-holy-sepulchre/
- **Vatican, St Peter's.** The Cross relic is kept in a dome pier. Vatican News (2024) says: "Da secoli è custodita in Vaticano insieme ai chiodi della crocefissione." Source: https://www.vaticannews.va/it/vaticano/news/2024-09/reliquie-maggiori-basilica-san-pietro-vaticano-giubileo.html
- **Etchmiadzin, Armenia.** The Mother See's Treasury-House lists "The Cross of Ashot the Iron, housed in a reliquary containing a relic of Christ's Cross (the oldest of the existing cross relics)". Source: https://www.armenianchurch.org/en/alex-marie-manoogian-museum. The "oldest" is the Mother See's own claim.
- **Genoa, Museo del Tesoro di San Lorenzo.** The museum lists "the Cross of the Zacharias, an ornate crucifix in which a relic of the true Cross is set". Source: https://www.museidigenova.it/en/node/634
- **Mount Athos, and the Greek Orthodox fragment at the Holy Sepulchre:** not researched, no source fetched **(to verify)**.

**Custodians:** Santa Croce's basilica and parish (Vicariate of Rome); the Archdiocese of Paris, with the cathedral chapter and the Knights of the Holy Sepulchre (see §4); the Custody of the Holy Land; the Fabbrica di San Pietro; the Mother See of Holy Etchmiadzin.

**First attestations, all primary and all fetched:**
- **Cyril of Jerusalem, *Catechetical Lectures*, c. 348–350.**
  - Cat. 4.10: "the whole world has since been filled with pieces of the wood of the Cross". Source: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/310104.htm
  - Cat. 10.19: "The holy wood of the Cross bears witness, seen among us to this day, and from this place now almost filling the whole world, by means of those who in faith take portions from it". Source: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/310110.htm
  - Cat. 13.4: "the wood of the Cross confutes me, which was afterwards distributed piecemeal from hence to all the world". Source: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/310113.htm
  - Cyril does **not** name Helena.
- **Egeria, *Itinerarium*, c. 381–384** (McClure & Feltoe translation, 1919). On Good Friday at Golgotha: "a silver-gilt casket is brought in which is the holy wood of the Cross. The casket is opened and (the wood) is taken out, and both the wood of the Cross and the title are placed upon the table… because, I know not when, some one is said to have bitten off and stolen a portion of the sacred wood, it is thus guarded by the deacons". Source: https://www.ccel.org/m/mcclure/etheria/etheria.htm
- **Ambrose, *De obitu Theodosii*, delivered 25 Feb 395.** This is the first narrative connecting Helena with the finding. Latin text from https://la.wikisource.org/wiki/De_obitu_Theodosii_oratio:
  - §45: "Aperit itaque humum, decutit pulverem: tria patibula confusa reperit…"
  - §46: "Invenit ergo titulum, Regem adoravit, non lignum utique; quia hic gentilis est error".
  - §47: "Quaesivit clavos, quibus crucifixus est Dominus, et invenit. De uno clavo frenos fieri praecepit, de altero diadema intexuit".
  - The delivery date and the observation that it is the first Helena narrative come from the Judaism and Rome (ERC project) commentary: https://www.judaism-and-rome.org/ambrose-milan-oration-death-theodosius-48. That page also notes that Eusebius "does not discuss the relics of the cross or connect Helena with this information". **[DISAGREEMENT, among the ancient sources]** Eusebius is silent on the finding. Ambrose (395) says the *titulus* identified the Cross. Rufinus and others say the identification was a miracle. The CCEL editors' footnote records this: "other authorities, including Rufinus, state that it was found in a separate place from the cross, and that the recognition of the true cross was due to a miracle."

**Key dated events:**
- **Persian capture of Jerusalem.**
  - 614: Custody page ("The conquest of Jerusalem by the Persians in 614… the relic of the True Cross was stolen") and *Catholic Encyclopedia*, "Jerusalem (A.D. 71-1099)", https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/08355a.htm ("In 614 they stormed Jerusalem… St. Helena's great relic of the Holy Cross was taken off to Persia in triumph").
  - **[DISAGREEMENT, date]** The *CE* "Holy Lance" article gives "In 615 Jerusalem was captured": https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/08773a.htm
- **Heraclius restores the Cross.**
  - Custody page: "brought back to Jerusalem by the Byzantine Emperor Heraclius in **630**".
  - **[DISAGREEMENT, date]** *CE* "Jerusalem": "In **629** Heraclius himself came to Jerusalem to venerate the Cross. This is the origin of the feast of the Exaltation of the Holy Cross (14 September…)". The feast is an act of cult.
- **Hattin, 4 July 1187.** The crusader relic was lost. De Re Militari's "Four Accounts" (translated primary sources, https://deremilitari.org/2014/01/the-battle-of-hattin-1187-four-accounts/):
  - Old French Continuation of William of Tyre (Lyon *Eracles*): "The Holy Cross also was lost." The same text records that a Templar later claimed to have buried it: "They went secretly and dug for three nights but could not find anything."
  - Letter to Archumbald, Hospitaller master in Italy: "The king was captured and the Holy Cross."
- **Santa Croce's portion.** The *Liber Pontificalis* says Constantine gave a portion to the Sessorian basilica. The *CE* "The True Cross" (Cabrol, 1908) reports this only "if we are to accept the authenticity of its statement": https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/04529a.htm

**Scientific tests:** none on the Cross wood were located. The Titulus was tested; see §2.

**Formal Church acts about the object:**
- **None located that declares any fragment authentic.**
- Second Council of Nicaea (787) *(adjacent, doctrinal)*. As summarised by *CE* "True Cross", it defined that veneration is due "to the form 'of the precious and vivifying cross'". This concerns the cross as a form, not any fragment.
- Feasts of the Invention (3 May) and Exaltation (14 Sept) *(adjacent)*.
- **(to verify)** A search-engine summary says John Paul II called the Santa Croce chapel "the true 'sanctuary of the Cross'" on 25 March 1979. I did not fetch it, and it would be a cult act in any case.

**Main disagreement: the volume of the fragments. [DISAGREEMENT]**
- **Calvin, *Traité des reliques* (Geneva, 1543)**, from https://www.info-bible.org/histoire/reforme/traite-des-reliques-jean-calvin.htm: "Bref, si on voulait ramasser tout ce qui s'en est trouvé, il y en aurait la charge d'un bon grand bateau. L'Evangile testifie que la croix pouvait être portée d'un homme. Quelle audace donc a-ce été de remplir la terre de pièces de bois en telle quantité que trois cents hommes ne les sauraient porter !"
- **Charles Rohault de Fleury, *Mémoire sur les instruments de la Passion de N.-S. J.-C.* (Paris: L. Lesort, 1870)**, full text from archive.org (https://archive.org/details/mmoiresurlesin00rohauoft).
  - He frames his inquiry against Calvin: "si Calvin, en un mot, a bien calculé lorsqu'il affirme que ses restes produisent un volume cent fois supérieur à cet instrument de supplice."
  - His conclusion: "le volume total des reliques qui nous sont parvenues est de 5 millions de millimètres environ… en triplant pour l'inconnu le volume connu. On arrive ainsi à 15 millions de millimètres qui ne font pas le dixième des 180 millions de millimètres que nous trouverons pour le volume de la croix".
- **Secondary-report discrepancy. [DISAGREEMENT]** *CE* "True Cross" (1908) restates Rohault's figures as "above 4,000,000 cubic millimetres" known and "178,000,000" for the whole cross. That does not match Rohault's own "5 millions" and "180 millions" at the page fetched. Also, the attack Cabrol rebuts ("be comparable in bulk to a battleship") is not Calvin's wording; Calvin wrote "un bon grand bateau". Record both. Neither author's calculation is established fact.

## 2. Titulus Crucis

**Location as of 2026:** Santa Croce in Gerusalemme, Rome, in the same case as the Cross relics. The basilica page says: "Nella stessa teca è conservato anche Il Titulus Crucis – la tavoletta reliquia della Croce probabilmente portata nella Basilica nel VI secolo e rinvenuta nel XV secolo murata all'interno dell'arco absidale". The chapel was closed from 16 Apr 2026 (see §1). Source: https://www.santacroceroma.it/it/features-2/reliquie.html

**Custodian:** the Basilica of Santa Croce in Gerusalemme. The Radiocarbon paper's acknowledgements name "Rev Simone Fioraso, Prior of the Basilica".

**First attestation:**
- A "title" was shown with the Cross wood in Jerusalem c. 381–384 (Egeria; see §1). Nothing ties that object to the Rome tablet.
- Ambrose (395) has Helena find the *titulus*.
- The Rome tablet's own documented history starts **1 Feb 1492**. Leiden Medievalists Blog, "Fake news in fifteenth-century Rome" (https://www.leidenmedievalistsblog.nl/articles/fake-news-in-fifteenth-century-rome-the-miraculous-discovery-of-the-titulus): Stefano Infessura wrote "That day there was a miracle in the city", and "inside a small hollow in the roof, a leaden box was discovered. A marble plaque on top of the box had the inscription HIC EST TITULUS VERAE CRUCIS".
- **[DISAGREEMENT, among the contemporary accounts]** The same article: "three more reports by contemporaries, Johannes Burckhard, Leonardo Sarzana, and Sigismondo dei Conti… give slightly different versions… of how, when, and where the relic was discovered, as well as what its container looked like."
- **[DISAGREEMENT, where it was found]** The basilica says "murata all'interno dell'arco absidale". Infessura, as reported by Leiden, says "a small hollow in the roof".

**Key figure, Cardinal Pedro González de Mendoza (1422–1499).** Leiden: "the titular Cardinal of Santa Croce, Pedro Gonzáles de Mendoza (1422-1499), was the patron of the then ongoing reconstruction works… The 'discovery' of the Titulus relic was most likely presented at this moment because this would add further sparkle to the news of the Spanish victory [Granada]". That is the blog author's reading; attribute it to the author.

**Scientific test.** F. Bella & C. Azzi, "¹⁴C Dating of the 'Titulus Crucis'", *Radiocarbon* 44(3), 2002, pp. 685–689, doi:10.1017/S0033822200032136, Dipartimento di Fisica "E. Amaldi", Roma Tre. I read the full PDF (https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/E0FFBA7E6A68A5B7196ECF3F318C2D8C/S0033822200032136a.pdf/14c-dating-of-the-titulus-crucis.pdf).
- The object: "a single piece of walnut wood (species Juglans Regia L)", about 26 × 14 × 4 cm, 687 g.
- The conclusion, quoted exactly: "Radiocarbon age of the 'Titulus Crucis' = 1020 ± 30 BP. Calendar age… = AD 996–1023 (1 σ), AD 980–1146 (2 σ)… calculated using the INTCAL98 program… This dating permits one to consider alternative hypotheses regarding the origin of the tablet, such as the possibility that it is a copy of the original, as discussed by Rigato (2002)."
- Critiques and alternative readings: the paper points to Maria Luisa Rigato's copy hypothesis (Gregorian University doctorate, 2002). That work was not fetched. Anyone arguing for first-century authenticity against the ¹⁴C result: none fetched **(to verify)**.

**Formal Church act about the object:** none located. I checked the basilica's relic page, Vatican News 2023 (https://www.vaticannews.va/it/chiesa/news/2023-03/la-croce-testimonianza-viva-di-cristo.html), the Leiden article and the Radiocarbon paper. Some circulating accounts cite a 1496 bull of Alexander VI. **Not located; do not assert it (to verify).**

**Disambiguation:** the Titulus is not the True Cross. It is a separate object with a separate first attestation (1492 in Rome), and it has been dated by radiocarbon; the Cross wood has not.

## 3. Holy Nails (brief)

- **Rome, Santa Croce.** The basilica page: "furono sicuramente portate da sant'Elena a Roma… i frammenti della Vera Croce ed uno dei chiodi utilizzati nella Crocefissione". The basilica says "sicuramente"; that is its own claim.
- **Paris, Notre-Dame.** Diocese of Paris: "Le Clou trouve ses origines dans le trésor du Saint-Sépulcre. Le Patriarche de Jérusalem le remit… à l'empereur Charlemagne en 799… remit en 1824 à l'archevêque de Paris. D'une longueur de 9 cm".
- **Monza, Iron Crown.** Museo e Tesoro del Duomo di Monza, https://www.museoduomomonza.it/en/iron-crown/:
  - "the Crown is composed of six gold plates… bearing inside a metal circle… identifies with one of the nails used for the crucifixion of Christ".
  - "Recent scientific investigations suggest that the Crown… derives from interventions made between the IV-V and the IX century, may be a royal emblem of late antiquity, perhaps Ostrogoth".
  - **[DISAGREEMENT]** Secondary sources report a **1993** analysis finding the inner band to be silver, not iron. That appears only in search-engine summaries; I did not fetch a primary report **(to verify)**.
  - **Church act (adjacent, cult).** Museum: "in 1576 San Carlo Borromeo established the cult of the Sacred Nail, in order to make official the recognition of the diadem as a relic". This is an archbishop's act of cult on the object, as the museum characterises it. Innocent VI, 1354: the Cathedral's right to host coronations. That act concerns the church, not the object's authenticity.
- **Vatican.** Nails are kept with the Cross relic (Vatican News 2024).
- **Vienna.** The Holy Lance's central pin is venerated as a Nail; see §5.
- **Bamberg, Trier:** not researched **(to verify)**.
- **Ancient root:** Ambrose §47 (two nails, made into a bridle and a diadem).
- **Critique.** *CE* "Holy Nails" (Thurston), https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/10672a.htm: "Very little reliance can be placed upon the authenticity of the thirty or more holy nails which are still venerated… in such treasuries as that of Santa Croce in Rome, or those of Venice, Aachen, the Escurial, Nuremberg, Prague, etc. Probably the majority began by professing to be facsimiles which had touched or contained filings from some other nail". This is a Catholic reference work carrying an imprimatur, and it is sceptical.

## 4. Crown of Thorns

**Location as of 2026:** Notre-Dame de Paris, in the axial chapel behind the choir, in the châsse-reliquaire by Sylvain Dubuisson. Diocese of Paris (https://dioceseparis.fr/-la-sainte-couronne-d-epines-de-.html): "Chaque vendredi, la Couronne d'épines est présentée en ostension dans son reliquaire de 15h à 18h30. Le premier vendredi du mois, elle est proposée à la vénération des fidèles dans la nef".

**Custody:** the Archdiocese of Paris and the cathedral chapter. The Diocese says the relics are "confiées aux chanoines du Chapitre chargés de leurs vénérations et placées sous la garde statutaire des Chevaliers du Saint-Sépulcre de Jérusalem".

**Disambiguation, Sainte-Chapelle vs Notre-Dame.** Louis IX built the Sainte-Chapelle to house the Crown, which stayed there until the Revolution. It has been in the Notre-Dame cathedral treasury since 10 Aug 1806. The Sainte-Chapelle (a state monument) no longer holds it.

**First attestation:** *CE* "Crown of Thorns" (Thurston, 1908), https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/04540b.htm: "the 'Breviarius', and the 'Itinerary' of Antoninus of Piacenza, both of the sixth century, clearly state that the Crown of Thorns was at that period shown in the church upon Mount Sion". The *CE* describes Gregory of Tours' claim about the thorns' miraculous greenness as not strengthening "the historical testimony".

**Key dated events** (Diocese of Paris unless stated):
- **Between the 7th and 10th centuries**, the relics were moved to Constantinople.
- **1238**: "Baudouin II de Courtenay… propose au roi de France Louis IX… de lui engager la Couronne d'épines… les régents de l'Empire ont déjà mis les reliques en gage auprès de banquiers vénitiens que saint Louis dédommagera".
- **10 Aug 1239**: received at Villeneuve-l'Archevêque. **19 Aug 1239**: arrival in Paris; the king carried the Crown barefoot to Notre-Dame.
- **26 Apr 1248**: consecration of the Sainte-Chapelle. Sources: Herodote.net, https://www.herodote.net/26_avril_1248-evenement-12480426.php; *CE* has "completed 1248".
- **Revolution**: to Saint-Denis, then the Bibliothèque nationale. **1804**: handed to the Archbishop. **10 Aug 1806**: assigned to the cathedral treasury.
- **1896**: crystal-and-gold reliquary by Poussielgue-Rusand after Astruc.
- **15 Apr 2019 fire**: rescued. Europe 1 (https://www.europe1.fr/societe/laumonier-des-sapeurs-pompiers-sauve-la-couronne-depines-du-christ-de-notre-dame-elle-nappartient-pas-seulement-aux-chretiens-mais-a-lhumanite-3893535): "Jean-Marc Fournier, aumônier des sapeurs-pompiers de Paris, a contribué à la sauvegarde de la couronne d'épines". His own account was given on air. News coverage credits a human chain of firefighters and police; per sourcing rule 5, Fournier's role rests on his own testimony.
- **10 Apr 2020** (Good Friday): Archbishop Aupetit venerated the Crown in the damaged cathedral (Diocese).
- **7 Dec 2024**: the cathedral reopened.
- **Reliquary installation / first veneration:**
  - **Friday 13 Dec 2024.** Diocese: "Le vendredi 13 décembre 2024… Mgr Laurent Ulrich, archevêque de Paris, a présidé le retour de la Couronne d'épines à Notre-Dame de Paris."
  - *Le Journal des Arts* (https://www.lejournaldesarts.fr/patrimoine/une-nouvelle-chasse-reliquaire-notre-dame-175908): "Le soir du vendredi 13 décembre, la couronne a pris place dans la demi-sphère bleue… où elle ne sera visible qu'à certaines occasions."
  - **(to verify)** A search-engine summary gives Tuesday 19 Nov 2024 for installation of the châsse (the empty case). Not confirmed in a fetched page.
  - **First public venerations, 2025.** Ordre du Saint-Sépulcre (https://www.ordredusaintsepulcre.fr/articles/175640-venerations-de-la-sainte-couronne-depines-2025): "Du 10 janvier au vendredi Saint 18 avril, la Sainte Couronne d'épines est présentée à la vénération… chaque vendredi… À partir de mai 2025… le premier vendredi de chaque mois".

**Scientific tests:** none located. The Diocese describes the object as a ring of bundled rushes, from which the thorns were distributed. *CE*: "M. de Mély has been able to enumerate more than 700 such relics" of individual thorns. *CE* also says "That all the reputed holy thorns… cannot by any possibility be authentic will be disputed by no one."

**Formal Church act about the object:** none on authenticity located. Diocese and cathedral acts are all cult (*adjacent*): the returns and ostensions presided over by archbishops.

**Access note:** notredamedeparis.fr returned **403** to every fetch (bot filter; inconclusive, not dead). For Notre-Dame facts, the Diocese of Paris is the cited authority.

## 5. Holy Lance: several objects, never merge them

**Disambiguation. There are at least four "Holy Lances", each a separate object with a separate history:** (a) Vatican; (b) Vienna (the Imperial "Heilige Lanze"); (c) Etchmiadzin ("Geghard"); (d) Kraków (a copy of Vienna). The Antioch lance of 1098 is (e); its identity with any of the others is disputed.

**(a) Vatican, St Peter's, Longinus pier.**
- Vatican News 2024: "una parte dell'asta con la quale il centurione romano trafisse il costato di Cristo. Conservata nel tesoro sacro di Costantinopoli, fu donata a Papa Innocenzo VIII Cybo dal sultano Bajazet… nel 1492… La Sacra Lancia è offerta alla venerazione ancora oggi nella seconda settimana di Quaresima". The Lenten exposition is *adjacent*.
- The pier niches were adapted by Bernini under Urban VIII.
- **[DISAGREEMENT, already in 1492]** *CE* "Holy Lance", https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/08773a.htm: "great doubts as to its authenticity were felt at Rome, as Burchard's 'Diary'… plainly shows, on account of the rival lances known to be preserved at Nuremberg, Paris, etc., and on account of the supposed discovery… at Antioch".
- Benedict XIV compared a Paris drawing of the point with the Vatican relic and "was satisfied that the two had originally formed one" (*CE*, citing *De Beat. et Canon.* IV). **This is a scholarly opinion in a pope's treatise, not a decree about authenticity.**

**(b) Vienna, Kaiserliche Schatzkammer (Kunsthistorisches Museum), Hofburg.** Object record, https://www.khm.at/kunstwerke/die-heilige-lanze-100443:
- "Zeit: 8. Jh., Silbermanschette: 2. Hälfte 11. Jh; Goldmanschette: 3. Viertel 14. Jh."
- The museum's own voice: "Frommes Wunschdenken machte den Mittelstift bald zu einem der Nägel… Im Laufe des 13. Jahrhunderts wurde sie schließlich zur Lanze des Longinus".
- Inscriptions include "LANCEA ET CLAVVS DOMINI".
- **Church act (adjacent).** Charles IV "setzte beim Papst die Einführung eines Feiertages zu Ehren von Lanze und Nagel durch", i.e. a papal feast. Cult, not a finding.
- **Scientific tests. [DISAGREEMENT]**
  - **Robert Feather, 2003** (documentary *The Spear of Christ*). *Time*, 8 June 2003 (https://time.com/archive/6894352/piercing-an-ancient-tale/): X-ray diffraction and fluorescence found "the main body of the spear to be medieval, dating to the 7th century at the earliest". The iron pin is "'con-sistent' in length and shape with a 1st century Roman nail". This is a TV-documentary result reported by a magazine; no peer-reviewed publication was located.
  - **Vienna Institute for Archaeological Science (VIAS), University of Vienna, 2003–2004**, published in F. Kirchweger (ed.), *Die Heilige Lanze in Wien* (Schriften des KHM 9, 2005). Project page: https://vias.univie.ac.at/forschung/archaeometallurgie/projekte/die-hl-lanze-des-kunsthistorischen-museums-wien/. "im Februar 2004 — erstmals wieder seit 1924 — die goldene und die silberne Manschette abgenommen". The blade has a "für die 2. Hälfte des 8. Jahrhunderts typischen Form"; it was "eine[r] ursprünglich gebrauchsfähigen Flügellanze des 8. Jahrhunderts".
  - Net: the museum and VIAS say 8th century (Carolingian). Feather is reported as saying "7th century at the earliest". The two do not strictly contradict each other, but they differ in emphasis. Carry both.

**(c) Etchmiadzin, Armenia.**
- The Mother See's Treasury-House (https://www.armenianchurch.org/en/alex-marie-manoogian-museum) lists "The Holy Lance of Geghard (the spear that pierced Christ), preserved in a silver reliquary". The Treasury-House opened 11 Oct 1982.
- *CE* (1908) cites de Mély that the Antioch lance is "probably… identical with the relic now jealously preserved at Etschmiadzin… never in any proper sense a lance, but rather the head of a standard". **[DISAGREEMENT]** Raynaldi and the Bollandists instead thought the Antioch lance was the one Bayezid sent to Rome.
- Shoghakat TV (https://www.shoghakat.am/en/telecasts/15589) reports the lance was brought out and shown to the press after protesters raised "blames regarding the sacred relics". The page is undated in my extract; its date is **(to verify)**.

**(d) Kraków.** *CE*: "One other remaining lance… is preserved at Cracow, but… it is impossible to trace its earlier history." That it is a copy of the Vienna lance (Otto III, c. 1000) was not verified from a fetched source **(to verify)**.

**(e) Antioch, June 1098, Peter Bartholomew.**
- Fordham Internet Medieval Sourcebook (https://sourcebooks.fordham.edu/source/cde-antioch.asp), Raymond d'Aguiliers' version: "the Lord… chose a certain poor peasant, Provençal by race… 'Andrew, apostle of God… has commanded me… to give back to you… the Lance which opened the side of the Saviour.'"
- *CE* calls it a "discovery under very questionable circumstances by the crusader Peter Bartholomew".
- Peter's 1099 ordeal by fire, and Adhemar's scepticism: not in a fetched source **(to verify)**.

## 6. Scala Sancta (Holy Stairs)

**Location as of 2026:** Pontificio Santuario della Scala Santa e Sancta Sanctorum, Piazza di San Giovanni in Laterano, Rome (https://www.scala-santa.com/). **Custodians:** the Passionist Fathers.

**Tradition vs history:**
- The sanctuary: "ventotto gradini che la tradizione ci tramanda come i gradini del palazzo di Pilato… Il loro arrivo a Roma è collegato con la storia della Regina Elena… (327-328)". Source: https://www.scala-santa.com/la-scala-santa/
- *CE* (https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/13505a.htm): "The historians of the monument relate that the Holy Stairs were brought… about 326 by St. Helena". The *CE* attributes this to "historians of the monument". In the Middle Ages they were called *Scala Pilati*. **1589**: Sixtus V moved them to their present site.
- **[DISAGREEMENT, date]** The sanctuary says 327–328. Musei Vaticani and *CE* say 326.

**Key events:**
- Wooden covering added: **1724** according to the sanctuary ("furono ricoperti di legno nel 1724"). **[DISAGREEMENT, date]** Musei Vaticani says "voluta nel 1723 da Papa Innocenzo XIII".
- **13 Jun 1853**, *Decet Romanum Pontificem*, and **24 Feb 1854**, *Inter plurima templa* (Pius IX): custody and property given to the Passionists. Entry on 28 Sep 1853. Source: https://www.scala-santa.com/chi-siamo/

**Formal Church acts (all adjacent):**
- Pius IX's two constitutions of 1853–54 concern custody and administration of the sanctuary.

- **None on the authenticity of the stairs.**

**Scientific tests:** none located. The marble was restored, not dated.

## 7. Column of the Flagellation: two rival claims, record both

**Claim A: Rome, Santa Prassede.** Vatican News 2023 (https://www.vaticannews.va/it/chiesa/news/2023-03/il-mistero-della-colonna-della-flagellazione-di-santa-prassede.html):
- "una colonnina di pietra bianca e nera", 63 cm, in a reliquary by Duilio Cambellotti (1898). The stone is "gabbro diorite… proveniente dalla parte settentrionale del Deserto orientale egiziano".
- "sarebbe stata portata da Gerusalemme a Roma dal cardinale Giovanni Colonna, durante la V Crociata, nel 1223".
- Vatican News itself flags the suspicion: "il cognome del legato pontificio… è lo stesso della reliquia e può essere letta in entrambi i modi: la prova che si tratti di un'invenzione… o, al contrario, un dono provvidenziale".

**Claim B: Jerusalem, Holy Sepulchre, Franciscan Chapel of the Apparition** (Custody of the Holy Land).
- Sanctuary page (https://www.custodia.org/en/sanctuaries/basilica-of-the-holy-sepulchre/): "the Column of the Flagellation, a piece of the red porphyry column, venerated for centuries by the Latin faithful in the Upper Room… brought to this location in **1553** by Father Custos Boniface of Ragusa."
- **[DISAGREEMENT, inside the Custody's own pages]** A Custody news page (https://www.custodia.org/en/news/holy-wednesday-jerusalem-veneration-column/) says: "The column was only taken to the Holy Sepulchre in the **14th century**."
- The rite is held each Holy Wednesday (*adjacent*): https://www.custodia.org/en/news/holy-wednesday-passio-and-veneration-column/
- **Ancient attestation of a column** (no identification with either surviving object): Egeria: "to Sion, to pray at the column at which the Lord was scourged" (CCEL).

**Material disagreement:** Rome's column is described as black-and-white gabbro-diorite; Jerusalem's as red porphyry. They are different stones, and the two claims are carried side by side. **Church act on either: none located.**

## Disambiguations

1. **Titulus ≠ True Cross.** They are separate objects with separate attestations. Only the Titulus has a ¹⁴C date (AD 980–1146, 2σ).
2. **Holy Lances.** Vatican (point-less blade; from Bayezid II, 1492), Vienna (8th-century winged lance with a "nail" pin), Etchmiadzin ("Geghard", a standard-head form), Kraków (copy), and the Antioch 1098 find. Identity claims between them are disputed (*CE*).
3. **Crown custody.** Sainte-Chapelle 1248 to the Revolution; Notre-Dame treasury since 10 Aug 1806; displaced by the 2019 fire; returned 13 Dec 2024. The Sainte-Chapelle does not hold it today.
4. **The two Flagellation columns.** Santa Prassede (gabbro-diorite, 1223) vs Holy Sepulchre (red porphyry, moved 1553 or 14th century). Different stones and different claims.
5. **"Holy Grail."** Valencia (agate cup) vs Genoa (green glass dish, shown in 1806 to be glass rather than emerald). Distinct objects.
6. **"Iron Crown" nail vs the other Holy Nails.** Monza's inner band is a separate claimant from the nails at Santa Croce and Notre-Dame and the Vienna pin.
7. **Cult acts vs authenticity findings (ADR-0007).** Papal masses using the chalice, jubilees, Lenten ostensions, feasts, custody bulls and papal gifts are all *adjacent*.
