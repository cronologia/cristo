# cronologia/cristo: research strand B (cloth, image and blood relics)

Research only. No repo files were touched. Fetches ran on 2026-09-24 through the session proxy (curl with a desktop-browser user agent). A reference below counts as "fetched" only if it returned HTTP 200 and I read the text quoted from it. Where I could read only metadata (Crossref) or a search-engine summary, the reference says so.

**How acts are classed (ADR-0007, applied to relics).** Every entry under "Church act" names the authority, the object and the document. Expositions, papal visits, indulgences, basilica titles and processions are acts of cult (**adjacent**). None of them is a finding of authenticity. **I did not locate any Church document that declares any relic in this strand authentic.** In three places the custodians themselves say the opposite (Turin, Trier, and the Turin Custodian's own site). One circulating "approval" claim turned out, on checking, to be a mis-read act: the Catholic Encyclopedia's "Clement VI … approved the exposition", covered under the Shroud of Turin below.

---

## 1. Shroud of Turin (Sindone)

**Location (2026):** Cathedral of San Giovanni Battista, Turin, Italy. It sits in "the last chapel of the left nave, under the Royal Tribune", sealed inside a conservation case within a metal chest and normally not visible [sindone-dove].
**Owner:** the Holy See. It passed to the Holy See by testamentary bequest when Umberto II of Savoy died on 18 March 1983 [sindone-custode]. sindone.org says: "decide il Papa, che è il proprietario della Sindone, lasciata in eredità alla Santa Sede da Umberto II di Savoia" ("the Pope decides, being the owner of the Shroud, which Umberto II of Savoy left to the Holy See") [sindone-dove].
**Custodian:** the Archbishop of Turin as Pontifical Custodian. As of 2026 that is Card. Roberto Repole, who succeeded Mons. Cesare Nosiglia [sindone-custode; sindone-home]. The Custodian is served by the Diocesan Commission for the Shroud, renewed for 2026–2031 [sindone-home]. His scientific support comes from the Centro Internazionale di Studi sulla Sindone (CISS), which "statutariamente assicura il suo supporto scientifico al Custode" ("under its statutes provides scientific support to the Custodian") [sindone-comunicato-2025].

**First unambiguous attestation:** the Lirey cloth of the 1350s.
- Catholic Encyclopedia, "Troyes" (1913): "On 20 June, 1353, Geoffroy de Charny … founded at Lirey … a collegiate church … and in this church he exposed for veneration the Holy Winding Sheet" [ce-troyes].
- Catholic Encyclopedia, "Holy Shroud" (Thurston): "we first hear of it about the year 1360" [ce-shroud].
- **DISAGREEMENT:** the documentary start is dated c.1353–57 by some and c.1360 by others. The task brief's "c.1354–55" is not quoted verbatim by any source I fetched.

**Key dated events:**
- **c.1355:** Bishop Henri de Poitiers holds an inquiry. What we know of it comes only from d'Arcis's later memorandum (below).
- **c.1389:** memorandum of Bishop Pierre d'Arcis of Troyes to the Avignon pope Clement VII. Thurston's 1903 translation (*The Month* 101: 17–29) is hosted on a partisan site [darcis-memo]. It reads: "procured for his church a certain cloth cunningly painted, upon which by a clever sleight of hand was depicted the twofold image of one man". Of Henri de Poitiers it says: "he discovered the fraud and how the said cloth had been cunningly painted, the truth being attested by the artist who had painted it". The same page describes the text as an "anonymous, unsigned, undated, unsealed, rough draft", citing BnF Collection de Champagne v.134 f.138 and v.154 f.137.
  - **DISAGREEMENT:** sindonologists dispute the memorandum's status (a draft, perhaps never sent). No scholarly source was fetched for that dispute; it was seen only in a search summary.
- **6 Jan 1390:** Clement VII (Avignon obedience) issues bulls. **This is the relabelling trap.** The two sources below are the same encyclopedia, and they contradict each other:
  - Catholic Encyclopedia "Troyes" (1913): "Clement VI by four Bulls, 6 Jan., 1390, approved the exposition as lawful" [ce-troyes]. The name is wrong: Clement VI died in 1352.
  - Catholic Encyclopedia "Holy Shroud" (Thurston): the pope, "without absolutely prohibiting the exhibition of the Shroud, decided after full examination that in the future when it was shown to the people, the priest should declare in a loud voice that it was not the real shroud of Christ, but only a picture made to represent it" [ce-shroud].
  - **Record both, and do not merge them.** The act permits exposition under conditions. It is not an approval of authenticity. **The bull texts themselves were not fetched,** so both statements stay attributed to the encyclopedia.
- **1418:** the canons entrust the cloth to Humbert de La Roche [ce-troyes].
- **1452 or 1453:** Margaret de Charny gives it to the Duke of Savoy. **DISAGREEMENT on the date:** CE "Troyes" says 1452 [ce-troyes]; Wikipedia says 1453 [wp-shroud-history].
- **1532:** fire at Chambéry. The burn lines and holes are "i danni dovuti all'incendio avvenuto a Chambéry nel 1532" ("the damage from the fire at Chambéry in 1532") [sindone-cose]. The day and month were not verified from a fetched primary source.
- **14 Sep 1578:** moved from Chambéry to Turin "per abbreviare il viaggio dell'Arcivescovo di Milano, Carlo Borromeo" ("to shorten the journey of the Archbishop of Milan, Carlo Borromeo"). This was the first exposition in Turin [sindone-1578].
- **25 May – 2 Jun 1898:** exposition. The lawyer Secondo Pia, "autorizzato da casa Savoia" ("authorised by the House of Savoy"), photographs the cloth for the first time, and the negative reveals details not seen before [sindone-1898].
- **Expositions in the 20th century** [sindone-ostensioni-900]:
  - 1931 (royal wedding)
  - 1933 (Holy Year)
  - 1969: private examination by Card. Pellegrino's commission, with the first colour photos
  - 1973: first live television exposition
  - 26 Aug – 8 Oct 1978: 4th centenary. Afterwards, "esami diretti (misure e osservazioni per 120 ore consecutive) compiuti da 44 studiosi" ("direct examinations (measurements and observations for 120 consecutive hours) carried out by 44 scholars"). The US STURP team was part of this. [sindone-1978]
  - 18 Apr – 14 Jun 1998
  - 12 Aug – 22 Oct 2000
- **WWII:** the cloth was kept at Montevergine (Avellino) [sindone-1978].
- **24 Feb 1993:** the cloth was moved provisionally to the cathedral choir. That move is why it escaped the **11–12 April 1997** fire, which destroyed Guarini's chapel. It was carried to the archbishop's palace, and an inspection on 14 April found "non aveva subito alcun danno" ("it had suffered no damage") [sindone-incendio].
- **2002:** conservation work by Mechthild Flury-Lemberg removed the 1534 Poor Clare patches [sindone-2010; sindone-ostensioni-900].
- **Later expositions:**
  - 10 Apr – 23 May 2010 ("Passio Christi, passio hominis"; Benedict XVI visits on 2 May) [sindone-2010]
  - 30 Mar 2013: televised exposition [sindone-ostensioni-900]
  - 19 Apr – 24 Jun 2015 (bicentenary of Don Bosco) [sindone-2015]
  - 9 Jul 2022: extraordinary exposition for the Taizé youth meeting [sindone-custode]
- **2025 Jubilee:** **no physical public exposition located** on sindone.org. What I found was a digital "Tenda di Avvolti" in Piazza Castello, open until "lunedì 5 maggio" (5 May 2025 was a Monday), "nell'ambito delle iniziative della Chiesa torinese per la Festa della Sindone e il Giubileo della Speranza" ("among the Turin Church's initiatives for the Feast of the Shroud and the Jubilee of Hope") [sindone-avvolti]. The site also says Pope Leo XIV was the first to try the "Avvolti" digital experience, at an audience with the Custodian [sindone-home].
- **Next exposition:** "nessuno sa quando sarà la prossima ostensione" ("no one knows when the next exposition will be") [sindone-dove].

**Scientific tests** (each result belongs to its authors):
- **STURP, 1978 examination; summary issued at a press conference in October 1981** (John Heller's text, hosted by the pro-authenticity site shroud.com) [sturp-summary]. It says: "No pigments, paints, dyes or stains have been found on the fibrils" and "the Shroud image is that of a real human form of a scourged, crucified man. It is not the product of an artist."
- **Walter McCrone** (critical): "The Shroud of Turin: blood or artist's pigment?", *Accounts of Chemical Research* 23 (1990) 77–83, doi 10.1021/ar00171a004. Read at metadata level only [mccrone-1990]. **DISAGREEMENT with STURP on the presence of pigment.**
- **Radiocarbon:** Damon et al., "Radiocarbon dating of the Shroud of Turin", *Nature* 337, 611–615, published 16 Feb 1989, doi 10.1038/337611a0; the laboratories were Arizona, Oxford and Zurich [nature-1989].
  - The Nature abstract: "The results provide conclusive evidence that the linen of the Shroud of Turin is mediaeval."
  - The full text, read in a copy hosted at shroud.com: "The age of the shroud is obtained as AD 1260-1390, with at least 95% confidence" [nature-1989-text].
- **Critiques** (record as critiques):
  - Rogers, "Studies on the radiocarbon sample from the shroud of turin", *Thermochimica Acta* 425 (2005) 189–194 (metadata only) [rogers-2005]. It argues the sample area was unrepresentative (the reweave hypothesis).
  - Casabianca, Marinelli, Pernagallo, Torrisi, "Radiocarbon Dating of the Turin Shroud: New Evidence from Raw Data", *Archaeometry* 61(5) 1223–1231, 22 Mar 2019, doi 10.1111/arcm.12467. The Wiley page is bot-walled (403, Cloudflare). The abstract, read via Crossref, says the raw data released in 2017 "strongly suggests that homogeneity is lacking in the data and that the procedure should be reconsidered" [casabianca-2019].
- **2025 newer claims:**
  - Moraes, "Image Formation on the Holy Shroud—A Digital 3D Approach", *Archaeometry* (online 28 Jul 2025), doi 10.1111/arcm.70030. It argues the contact pattern fits a low-relief model [moraes-2025].
  - The Custodian's communiqué of 4 Aug 2025 (Card. Repole) responds: "Il Custode della Sindone non ha motivo di entrare nel merito delle ipotesi formulate liberamente da scienziati più o meno accreditati" ("The Custodian of the Shroud has no reason to enter into the merits of hypotheses freely put forward by scientists of greater or lesser standing"). It refers readers to a CISS analysis [sindone-comunicato-2025].
  - Sarzeaud, "A New Document on the Appearance of the Shroud of Turin from Nicole Oresme…", *Journal of Medieval History* 51 (2025) 604–621, doi 10.1080/03044181.2025.2546884 (metadata only) [sarzeaud-2025].

**Church acts** (object named each time):
- **Clement VII (Avignon), 6 Jan 1390: bulls regulating exposition at Lirey (not authenticity).** See the Lirey disagreement above. Adjacent at most; the documents themselves were not fetched.
- **John Paul II, address in Turin Cathedral, 24 May 1998 (the Church's stated position on competence; not a finding)** [jp2-1998]:
  - "Since it is not a matter of faith, the Church has no specific competence to pronounce on these questions. She entrusts to scientists the task of continuing to investigate…"
  - "the Shroud is a mirror of the Gospel"
  - "this image, which everyone sees and no one at present can explain"
  - It describes the cloth as the sheet "which, according to tradition, wrapped the body of our Redeemer".
- **Benedict XVI, meditation in Turin, 2 May 2010 (cult: a pilgrimage during the exposition)** [b16-2010]: "The Shroud is the Icon of this mystery, the Icon of Holy Saturday", and "The Shroud is an Icon written in blood". This is a devotional reading, not a finding.
- **The custodian's own site (official):** "Questa tradizione, anche se ha trovato numerosi riscontri dalle indagini scientifiche sul Lenzuolo, non può ancora dirsi definitivamente provata" ("This tradition, although it has found considerable support in scientific investigations of the Cloth, cannot yet be said to be definitively proven") [sindone-cose].
- **Authenticity declaration: none located.** I checked vatican.va (both papal texts above), the Custodian's pages on sindone.org and the 2017 Instruction on relics. The 2017 Instruction concerns relics of Saints and Blessed only, which is a scope finding; see §9.

**Main disagreements:**
1. Medieval origin (Nature 1989; d'Arcis) versus authenticity (STURP 1981 summary; sindonologists).
2. The validity of the C-14 sample (Rogers 2005; Casabianca 2019).
3. What the 1390 bulls did (the two Catholic Encyclopedia articles).
4. The status of the d'Arcis memorandum.
5. The start date (1353/57 versus c.1360) and the handover date (1452 versus 1453).

## 2. Sudarium of Oviedo

**Location:** Cámara Santa, Oviedo Cathedral (Catedral de San Salvador), Oviedo, Asturias, Spain [oviedo-camara].
**Custodian:** the Cathedral Chapter of Oviedo / Archdiocese of Oviedo. This is inferred from the site owner; no named office-holder was verified.
**Tradition:** an ark of relics left Jerusalem in 614, travelled through Cartagena, Seville and Toledo, and was hidden on Monsacro before Alfonso II (791–842) brought it to Oviedo [oviedo-camara]. This is the cathedral's account and should be attributed to it.
**First unambiguous attestation:** the record drawn up at the solemn opening of the Arca Santa on **13 March 1075**, before Alfonso VI. Among the relics it lists "de sudario Domini" [oviedo-camara]. **DISAGREEMENT:** Wikipedia gives "March 14, 1075" [wp-sudarium].
**Key events** [oviedo-camara]:
- Alfonso VI has the ark covered in embossed silver.
- October 1934: the Cámara Santa is dynamited during the revolution; it is rebuilt in 1939–42.
- 9 Aug 1977: theft of the Cruz de los Ángeles, the Cruz de la Victoria and the Caja de las Ágatas. These are not the Sudarium; they were later recovered.

**Cult:** the Sudarium is used to bless the faithful three times a year: "el Viernes Santo, el 14 de septiembre … y el 21 de septiembre" ("Good Friday, 14 September … and 21 September") [ces-sudario-veneracion].
**Studies:**
- Mons. Giulio Ricci (1969) first linked it to the Shroud.
- Max Frei's pollen report (1979).
- Baima Bollone (1985) reported blood group AB.
- The EDICES team (Centro Español de Sindonología) has studied it since 1989 [ces-sudario-investigaciones; ces-about].

All of these come from a pro-authenticity association.

**Radiocarbon:** no journal publication located.
- Reported only through Nicolotti (2019, Baylor UP), quoted in Wikipedia: "Between 1990 and 1992 three independent datings … in C14 laboratories in Tucson and Toronto: all three fragments were dated between the sixth and ninth centuries AD, with the highest probability being in the eighth century. In 2007 a new carbon dating examination provided the same results" [wp-sudarium].
- Barta & Fanti (CRC Press 2022) has a chapter, "Dating of the Sudarium" (metadata only) [barta-fanti-2022].
- **Flag:** the dates need the primary lab reports before they enter data.

**DISAGREEMENT:** the C-14 results (c. 7th–8th century) versus the Jerusalem tradition and CES's claim that it is compatible with the Shroud. Nicolotti says the Sudarium "continues to be presented as authentic by Spanish sindonologists, with growing support from the local ecclesiastical authority" (quoted in [wp-sudarium]).
**Church act on authenticity:** none located (I checked catedraldeoviedo.com and linteum.com). The three annual blessings are cult (adjacent).

## 3. Veil of Veronica (Rome) and the Volto Santo of Manoppello

**St Peter's claim:**
- **Location:** St Peter's Basilica, Vatican City. The veil is shown from the Veronica loggia (pier).
- **Custodian:** the Fabbrica di San Pietro and the Chapter of St Peter's, under Card. Mauro Gambetti, archpriest [pv-veronica-2025; bsp-veronica-2026].
- **Exposition, annually on the 5th Sunday of Lent** (station liturgy):
  - Press release for 6 Apr 2025: "From the loggia of Veronica, there will be the display of the relic of the Holy Face (Veil of Veronica)" [pv-veronica-2025].
  - 22 Mar 2026: "the exposition of the Major Relic of the 'Holy Face' (the Veil of Veronica)" [bsp-veronica-2026].
  - Both are **cult (adjacent)**. The Vatican's own wording is "relic", which is not an authenticity finding.
- **First attestation:** Wikipedia (finding aid) says "Firm records of the Veil begin only in 1199" (Gerald of Wales, Gervase of Tilbury), followed by Innocent III's procession and indulgences in 1207 [wp-veronica]. Unverified against the primary texts.
- **Other acts:**
  - Innocent III, 1207: an indulgence (adjacent).
  - Paul V, letter of 7 Sep 1616, restricting copies to canons of St Peter's; Urban VIII ordering existing copies destroyed. Both reported by the Manoppello shrine [manoppello-veronica]; Urban VIII's 1629 date is given in [wp-veronica]. These regulate reproduction; they are not findings.

**Manoppello:**
- **Location:** Basilica Santuario del Volto Santo, Manoppello (Pescara), Italy.
- **Custodian:** the Capuchin Friars Minor of the Provincia Serafica "Immacolata Concezione", with a rector appointed by the Provincial [manoppello-home].
- **Benedict XVI, 1 Sep 2006: a "private pilgrimage" to the shrine (cult, adjacent)** [b16-manoppello-2006]. He speaks of "contemplating the image of the Holy Face". A grep of the text returns **no occurrence of "veil" or "relic"**; the positive control "Holy Face" appears several times. The visit is not an authenticity finding.
- **DISAGREEMENT:** the Manoppello shrine argues the Roman original disappeared in the early 17th century, since copies switch from open-eyed to closed-eyed around 1617. It cites Gerhard Wolf's doubts over whether the original is still at St Peter's [manoppello-veronica]. Wikipedia reports a theory that it was stolen during the rebuilding of 1506 [wp-manoppello]. **St Peter's continues to expose "the relic of the Holy Face".** Record both sides.

## 4. Holy Tunic: Trier (Heiliger Rock) and Argenteuil (Sainte Tunique)

**Trier:**
- **Location:** Trier Cathedral (Hoher Dom St. Peter), Heilig-Rock-Kapelle, Trier, Germany. Since the 1974 renovation it has lain flat "in einem Holzschrein aus dem Jahre 1891 … unter einem klimatisierten Glasschrein" ("in an 1891 wooden shrine … under a climate-controlled glass case"). It is not visible, and the chapel opens only on the Heilig-Rock-Tage [trier-dom-wallfahrten].
- **Custodian:** the Bishop and Diocese of Trier (Bishop Stephan Ackermann in 2012).
- **First attestation:** the Gesta Treverorum, for **1 May 1196**, when Archbishop Johann I consecrated the high altar of the new east choir and enclosed the tunic in it [trier-faq; trier-geschichte].
- **The diocese's own words:** the Helena story is legend: "Es wurde ein geschichtliches Ereignis erfunden, um einen Tatbestand zu erklären" ("A historical event was invented to explain a state of affairs") [trier-geschichte].
- **Events:**
  - 14 Apr 1512: the altar is opened before Emperor Maximilian I, followed by the first pilgrimage.
  - Wartime evacuations to Ehrenbreitstein, Würzburg, Bamberg and Augsburg; returned in 1810.
  - Moved to Limburg from 29 Aug 1939 to Oct 1944 [trier-geschichte].
- **Expositions/pilgrimages** [trier-dom-wallfahrten]: 1512–1517, 1524, 1531, 1538, 1545, 1655, 1810, 1844 (17 Aug – 6 Oct), 1891 (20 Sep – 4 Oct), 1933 (23 Jul – 10 Sep), 1959 (19 Jul – 20 Sep), 1996, and 2012 (13 Apr – 13 May).
  - **DISAGREEMENT on the list:** a search summary of the diocese's material included **1765**, but the cathedral page I fetched lists 1655 then 1810.
  - **DISAGREEMENT on 1996 attendance:** "über eine Million Pilger" ("over a million pilgrims") [trier-geschichte] versus "700.000 Pilger" [trier-faq]. These are two pages of the same diocese.
- **Textile finding (the diocese):** the present garment is a 16th-century carrier with an older woollen core "schon sehr alt (nicht genau datierbar)" ("already very old (not precisely datable)") [trier-faq]. No radiocarbon test located.
- **Church position (the custodian diocese):** "Die Frage nach der Echtheit des Heiligen Rockes kann nicht eindeutig beantwortet werden" ("The question of the Holy Robe's authenticity cannot be answered with certainty") [trier-dom-wallfahrten]. Authenticity act: **none; the custodian explicitly disclaims one.**

**Argenteuil:**
- **Location:** Basilique Saint-Denys, Argenteuil (Val-d'Oise), France, in a dedicated chapel where it can be venerated daily.
- **Custodian:** the parish, under the Bishop of Pontoise (Mgr Benoît Bertrand in 2025) [saintetunique-home; basilique-argenteuil].
- **Tradition:** a gift from Charlemagne to his daughter Théodrade, "arrivée le 12 août 800" ("arrived on 12 August 800") [saintetunique-home]. This is the shrine's account and should be attributed to it.
- **First attestation:** the **1156** charter, the "procès verbal de l'ostension" (formal record of the exposition), for an exposition organised by Hugh of Amiens, Archbishop of Rouen, in the presence of Louis VII [saintetunique-la-tunique].
- **Events** [saintetunique-la-tunique; wp-argenteuil]:
  - 18 Nov 1793: parish priest Ozet cuts the tunic into pieces to save it.
  - Stolen in Dec 1983 and returned on 2 Feb 1984, via Fr Guyard.
  - Classified as a monument historique on 23 May 1979.
- **Expositions:**
  - 25 Mar – 10 Apr 2016 (the Wikipedia date; the brief says 2016) [wp-argenteuil]
  - 18 Apr – 11 May 2025, for the Jubilee "sous l'autorité de Mgr Benoît Bertrand" ("under the authority of Mgr Benoît Bertrand") [saintetunique-home]
  - Both are cult (adjacent).
- **Radiocarbon:**
  - 2004, CEA/Saclay (LMC14): "Les résultats donnèrent une datation comprise en les années 530 et 650" ("The results gave a date between the years 530 and 650") [saintetunique-la-tunique]. The underlying report is Cottereau & Paterne, 26 May 2004 (cited in [wp-argenteuil]; not fetched).
  - LRMH catalogue record: the textile study by Isabelle Bédat includes a dating report marked "Confidentiel" [lrmh-bedat].
  - INA TV report, 8 Dec 2004: made "au 6ème siècle" ("in the 6th century") [ina-2004].
- **DISAGREEMENT:**
  - A 2005 test on another fragment, at Gérard Lucotte's initiative, gave 670–880; Wikipedia records that the scientific community rejects Lucotte's results [wp-argenteuil].
  - The shrine says "De nombreuses preuves d'authenticité ont été apportées" ("Numerous proofs of authenticity have been provided") [saintetunique-home] and that the C-14 researchers warned of contamination [saintetunique-la-tunique].
  - Nicolotti (quoted in [wp-sudarium]) notes that the tunic "has been C14 dated twice to the Middle Ages".
- **Authenticity act:** none located.

## 5. Image of Edessa / Mandylion (brief)

- **Genoa:** church of San Bartolomeo degli Armeni, where it has been "custodito e venerato da più di sei secoli" ("kept and venerated for more than six centuries").
- **Legend:** given by Christ to Abgar; moved to Constantinople in 945; given to Doge Leonardo Montaldo in the late 14th century.
- **Exhibition:** Museo Diocesano di Genova, 18 Apr – 18 Jul 2004. Catalogue by Wolf, Dufour Bozzo and Calderoni Masetti (Skira 2004), which reunited the panel with the Sinai wings of 945 [mandylion-2004]. Scholars treat the object as a Byzantine icon.
- **Vatican Mandylion** (Matilda chapel, formerly San Silvestro in Capite): **no source fetched. Flag it as "to verify".**
- **Authenticity act:** none located.

## 6. Holy Blood relics (brief)

**Bruges:**
- **Location:** Basilica of the Holy Blood (Heilig-Bloedbasiliek), Burg 13, Bruges, Belgium. Veneration is daily from 14:00 to 16:00 [holyblood-home].
- **Custodian:** the rector and the Noble Brotherhood of the Holy Blood [holyblood-home; unesco-bruges].
- **Origin, as the basilica tells it:** the story that Thierry of Alsace brought the relic in 1150 "wordt tegenwoordig door de meeste historici sterk betwijfeld" ("is today strongly doubted by most historians"). It is traced to the 14th-century chronicler Johannes Iperius. "De oudste betrouwbare bronnen over de relikwie situeren zich in de 13e eeuw" ("The oldest reliable sources on the relic date from the 13th century"), and historians link it instead to the sack of Constantinople in 1204 and Baldwin IX [holyblood-relic].
- **DISAGREEMENT:** UNESCO's text says the procession "dates back to the thirteenth century, when a citizen of Bruges is said to have brought the Relic … back from the Second Crusade" [unesco-bruges]. The Second Crusade was 1147–49, so the text is internally inconsistent, and it conflicts with the basilica's 1204 account.
- **1256:** the brief's "first attestation 1256" was **not found** in any fetched source. Flag it.
- **Procession:** "1291: Oudste vermelding van de processie" ("1291: oldest mention of the procession"), in a keure (by-law) of the porters' guild [holyblood-relic].
- **Clement V, bull of 1 Jun 1310 (object: pilgrimage to the relic, not authenticity; adjacent).** It encouraged pilgrimage to Bruges and is kept in the Bruges city archive. The basilica reports it; the document was not fetched [holyblood-relic].
- **UNESCO:** inscribed on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2009 (4.COM) [unesco-bruges]. The inscription concerns the procession, not the relic. The next procession is on 6 May 2027 [holyblood-home].

**Weingarten:**
- **The relic:** "Mit Christi Blut vermischte Erde" ("earth mixed with Christ's blood"). Tradition says it was found in Mantua on 12 Mar 1048; it came to Weingarten Abbey in 1094 via Judith of Flanders.
- **Location:** Basilica St. Martin, Weingarten, Germany.
- **Cult:** the Blutritt on the day after Ascension (adjacent) [weingarten-city]. The source is the municipality, repeating the tradition.

**Fécamp:**
- **Location:** Abbatiale de la Sainte-Trinité, Fécamp, France. Since the 16th century the relic has been in a tabernacle behind the high altar.
- **Legends (two competing):** the fig-tree trunk of Isaac, nephew of Joseph of Arimathea; or a eucharistic miracle at Saint-Léonard c.990.
- **Rediscovery:** "retrouvée au cours des travaux … vers 1170" ("found again during works … around 1170").
- **Confraternity:** founded in 1906 [fecamp-pelerinages]. The source is a devotional pilgrimage site.

## 7. Holy Prepuce of Calcata (brief, attributed)

Slate (2006, journalist's reporting) [slate-calcata]:
- The relic was found in Calcata in 1557, and a 10-year indulgence followed (adjacent).
- In 1983 the parish priest Dario Magnoni announced: "It has vanished. Sacrilegious thieves have taken it from my home." It had been kept "in a shoebox at the back of his wardrobe".
- Slate reports that a 1900 Vatican decree and a 1954 decision threatened excommunication for writing or speaking about it. **Neither document was located.** Treat both as unverified claims about Church acts; they are the kind of "Church decree" claim that ADR-0007 says must be traced to the document.
- Theories that the Vatican took it are local speculation reported by the journalist, and must be attributed as such.

## 8. Figures

- **Geoffroi de Charny** (d. 1356), knight and lord of Lirey. He founded the Lirey collegiate church on 20 Jun 1353, where the cloth was shown [ce-troyes].
- **Pierre d'Arcis**, Bishop of Troyes. Memorandum c.1389 [darcis-memo; ce-shroud].
- **Secondo Pia** (1855–1941), lawyer and amateur photographer. First photograph of the Shroud, in 1898 [sindone-1898].
- **Walter McCrone** (1916–2002), microscopist. Argued the image was iron-oxide/vermilion paint [mccrone-1990]. DISAGREES with STURP.
- **Mechthild Flury-Lemberg**, textile conservator. Led the 2002 Shroud restoration [sindone-ostensioni-900].

## 10. Disambiguations

- **Shroud vs Sudarium.**
  - Shroud: a full-body linen of about 4.41 × 1.13 m bearing a double image [sindone-cose].
  - Sudarium: a face cloth with stains and no image. The distinction rests on John 20's "linen cloths" and "sudarium" [ces-sudario-investigaciones].
  - Different cities and custodians. The claim that the two cloths are compatible is CES's, not a finding.
- **Veronica vs Manoppello vs Mandylion.**
  - Veronica: the Roman veil from the Way of the Cross, at St Peter's.
  - Manoppello: a veil the Capuchin shrine identifies with the lost Veronica, which St Peter's does not concede.
  - Mandylion: the Edessa/Abgar image, a painted panel at Genoa, plus a Vatican example.
  - Three different legends and objects.
- **Trier vs Argenteuil tunics.**
  - Trier: a woollen core inside a 16th-century tunic form.
  - Argenteuil: fragments of a wool tunic.
  - Both are identified with John 19:23–24 by their shrines. Neither custodian claims the other is false.
- **"First-class relic".** This is traditional devotional terminology. The **2017 Instruction** of the Congregation for the Causes of Saints uses instead "significant relics" and "non-significant relics", and it concerns "the Blesseds and … the Saints" [relics-instruction-2017]. A grep of the Instruction found 0 lines containing "class", while the positive control "significant" is present. **It does not regulate relics of Christ.** Its certificate rule (relics may not be displayed "without a proper certificate of the ecclesiastical authority who guarantees their authenticity") is written for relics of saints. Do not cite it as a Church certification of Passion relics.
- **"Icon" (Benedict XVI) and "mirror of the Gospel" (John Paul II)** are devotional descriptions. They are not verdicts on authenticity in either direction.
